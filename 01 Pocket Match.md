# 01 Pocket Match

Back to [[00 Paper Apps]].

A solo match-3 the size of a phone. She swaps two pieces, lines of three or more come off, the column falls, and sometimes the fall makes another line on its own. Twelve levels, one for each month of the year. The board, the rule card, and the level cards are paper. The pieces are buttons.

Working title until you rename it. Better names are private: something you call her phone habit, or a word from a particular night. The title on the box can be ordinary. The levels do the personal work.

## Why this shape

Candy Crush and Bejeweled are the same machine.

| Beat on the phone | On the table |
|---|---|
| A grid of a few colors | An 8×8 board, often masked down to 6×6, and five kinds of button |
| Flick two neighbors | Pick up two buttons and trade them |
| Three in a row disappear | She takes them off |
| Pieces fall, gaps fill from the top | She slides the column down and draws new buttons from an envelope |
| A lucky second match | Same, and it is free |
| Four in a row makes a striped candy | That button is flipped over and marked as a stripe |
| Five in a row makes a color bomb | Flipped and marked as a bomb |
| Jelly, ice, ingredients, a move limit | Drawn on that month's card |
| Next level | The next card |

You drop the timer, the lives, and the shop. Any legal swap is good enough. Hunting for the perfect move is what makes paper match-3 feel like homework, and it is not how these games actually feel on a phone.

## What you are building

One tin, or a deep envelope, that holds:

| Piece | Count | Make it from |
|---|---|---|
| Board | 1 | Heavy paper, 8×8, cells about 16–18 mm. Whole board about 15 cm square. Arrow in the margin pointing to the near edge: that direction is down. |
| Bezels | 2 | Paper frames that cover the board down to 7×7 and 6×6. Play area is the top-left square, so "down" still matches the arrow. |
| Buttons | 20 each of 5 colors (100) | Flat buttons, wood discs, or a 3/4 inch circle punch and colored card. Flat matters. Glass gems roll off a paper grid. |
| Icon | 1 per button | The same small drawing on every button of a color, in pen. Five drawings total. |
| Stripe / bomb | the back of any button | Blank until a match creates one. Pencil on the back: `—` or `\|` for a stripe, `*` for a bomb. Erase when it is spent. |
| Jelly | a small stack | Confetti squares, or a hole punch in a sixth color. They sit in the cell, under the button. |
| Bag | 1 | A small envelope labeled with the gravity arrow, holding every button not on the board. |
| Level cards | 12 | Index cards. Front is the puzzle. Back is one sentence. |
| Rule card | 1 | The turn, the matches, and the three obstacles. The long version can live in this note. |
| Envelope | 1 | Sits under the board. She opens it after level 12. |

Prototype the rules tonight before you punch a hundred circles: removable color-coding dots on a board slipped into a plastic sleeve. Peel, swap, restick. If the turn feels good with stickers, spend the time on buttons.

### The five icons

List fifteen small recurring things from the year. Keep five that can be drawn in a few seconds and that she would recognize. Assign each a color.

A stranger recognizing the drawing means it is too generic. Her recognizing it is the whole test. Draw them a little badly. That reads as made, which is what you want.

Leave one color slightly more common in the way you talk about the year, and use that color as the "ingredient" on the collect level.

## Rules

### Setup

1. Lay the bezel the card asks for.
2. Put jelly squares in the marked cells. Two squares in a cell means two-layer jelly.
3. Ice, if the card has any: buttons face down in those cells. A face-down button is frozen. It cannot be swapped and it does not fall.
4. Fill every other open cell from the envelope, random.
5. Settle the board before move 1. If any line of three or more is already sitting there, clear it, drop the column, refill, and keep going until the board is quiet. This costs no moves.
6. If the card is a collect level, name the ingredient color out loud and set a small dish beside the board.

### A turn

She swaps two buttons that share a side. Diagonals do not count.

If the swap does not make a line of three or more, she swaps back. That costs nothing. A bomb is the exception: swapping a bomb with a neighbor is always legal.

If it does make a line, she resolves the match, then gravity, then any chain, and that whole sequence costs one move.

She may take back a finished move, including the gravity, as often as she likes. Once a level she may lift any one ordinary button off the board and let gravity happen. That is the hammer, and it is free. Write both permissions on the rule card so the gift does not feel like a test.

### Matches

Lines are straight, in a row or a column. A line of three that turns a corner does not count unless each arm is its own line.

| She makes | What comes off | What stays |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | All three | Nothing |
| 4 in a straight line | The other three | The button she swapped into the line, flipped, with `—` if the line was horizontal or `\|` if it was vertical |
| 5 in a straight line, or a T or L made of five | The others | The button at the crossing or at the swap, flipped, marked `*` |
| Two lines at once | Everything in either line | One special, at the shared cell. A line of 5 or a T/L outranks a line of 4, which outranks nothing. |

A stripe or a bomb that was part of the cleared line fires before anything falls.

### Specials

**Stripe.** When it fires, every button in its row (`—`) or its column (`\|`) comes off, including the stripe. Jelly under those cells clears one layer. Ice touched by that line flips face up and stays.

**Bomb.** She swaps it with a neighbor. Every button of the neighbor's color comes off, plus the bomb. Jelly under those cells clears one layer.

**Stripe swapped with stripe.** Both fire. Row and column, or two rows, or two columns, depending on how they were marked.

**Bomb swapped with a stripe.** Clear every button of one color (she chooses), and also fire that stripe.

**Bomb swapped with bomb.** Clear the board. Treat it as a treat if it ever happens. Do not design a level that requires it.

Spent specials are erased and return to the envelope as ordinary buttons of their color.

### Gravity

After the clears:

1. In each column, slide every face-up button toward the arrow until it sits on the bottom, on a frozen button, or on another button.
2. Frozen buttons do not move. Buttons above them stack on top.
3. Fill empty cells from the top by drawing from the envelope.
4. If any new line of three or more exists, clear it the same way. This is a cascade. It does not cost a move.
5. Keep going until the board is quiet.

On a collect level, add a beat after step 2: any ingredient button sitting on the bottom row is lifted into the dish. Those cells are empty and get filled in step 3. Then check for cascades.

### Obstacles and goals

Use one main goal per level.

| Obstacle or goal | How it works | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| Jelly | A paper square under the button. A match, stripe, or bomb that includes that cell removes one square. | No squares left |
| Two-layer jelly | Two squares in the cell | Both have been removed, one clear at a time |
| Ice | Face-down button. A match in a neighboring cell, or a stripe/bomb that reaches it, flips it face up. It does not clear on the flip. | The card's count are face up, or the card's jelly under them is gone |
| Score | 10 points per ordinary button cleared. Each button a stripe or bomb clears is also 10. The second cascade in a turn is worth double, the third triple, and so on. Specials created are not extra points. | She reaches the number on the card |
| Collect | One color, named on the card. Bottom-row ingredients go to the dish after each fall. | The dish has the count on the card |
| Moves | Tally them on the card in pencil, or count out that many paper clips and return one per swap. | She hits the goal with clips left, or the clips run out |

If no swap on the board can make a line, she gathers every face-up button, puts them back in the envelope, and refills. Frozen buttons stay. This is free.

She wins by meeting the goal. Leftover moves are not a score that matters. If the clips run out first, the level is simply unfinished: reshuffle and try again, or add three moves. The rule card should say that in plain language.

### Playing beside her

Default is solo, the way the phone works.

If you want a role on the night you give it to her: you read the card, hold the envelope, and slide the columns while she chooses every swap. That is closer to sitting next to someone playing on their phone than a competitive board game is.

A real co-op variant, only if she asks: alternate swaps, shared goal, shared moves. Either person may point.

## The twelve levels

Play each one yourself before you write it on a card. The move counts below are drafts. Tuning rule: if you finish with more than five moves left, cut three moves or add a little jelly. If you fail twice on a relaxed try, add three moves. Err toward her finishing.

Every card front: level number, board size, color count (which colors to leave in the tin), moves, goal, and a tiny map if there is jelly or ice. Every card back: one specific sentence from that month. Not "I love you." A place, a meal, a sentence she said, a Tuesday.

Colors listed as 4 means you leave out whichever icon is least useful for that memory. Say on the card which one is sitting out, so the set still feels chosen.

| Lv | Board | Colors | Moves | Goal | Introduced here |
|----|-------|--------|-------|------|-----------------|
| 1 | 6×6 | 4 | 12 | Clear 6 jelly, scattered, none adjacent | Swap, match, fall. No specials yet. If she makes a line of 4, it just clears. |
| 2 | 6×6 | 4 | 14 | Clear 10 jelly | Cascades. Tell her on the card that a fall which makes a new line is the good part, and it is free. |
| 3 | 6×6 | 5 | 15 | Score 500 | The fifth icon, and scoring. 500 is a draft; replace it with about 80% of your playtest score. |
| 4 | 6×6 | 4 | 14 | Clear 8 jelly | Stripes. Put the jelly so a single line of 3 cannot finish the card; a stripe can. |
| 5 | 7×7 | 5 | 16 | Flip 8 ice | Ice. Spread the face-down buttons. No jelly yet, so the goal stays one sentence. |
| 6 | 7×7 | 5 | 16 | Score 800, draft from your playtest | Bombs. Halfway card. The sentence on the back can be longer than the others. |
| 7 | 7×7 | 4 | 15 | Collect 8 of one icon | Ingredients. Use the icon you chose as the "kept" thing. |
| 8 | 7×7 | 5 | 18 | Clear 8 double-jelly cells | Two-layer jelly. Eight cells, not a board full. |
| 9 | 8×8 | 5 | 20 | Clear the shape below, one layer | First full board. The shape is the goal, so do not also demand a score. |
| 10 | 8×8 | 4 | 18 | Score, set from your playtest | Open combo level. Four colors make cascades more likely. This is the juicy one. |
| 11 | 8×8 | 5 | 18 | Flip 6 ice and clear the single jelly under each | Ice and jelly together. Still one goal: those six cells end face up and empty of jelly. |
| 12 | 6×6 | 4 | 10 | Clear 5 jelly | Short on purpose. The back of the card says to look under the board. |

Level 9 map. `o` is jelly, `.` is a normal cell. Thirty-four jelly cells is a long level; if your playtest drags, delete the outer ones and keep the silhouette.

Level 12 should take a few minutes and should be hard to lose. The envelope underneath is the actual gift: the note, and whatever else is small enough to lie flat. The game is how she gets there, and it is also the thing she keeps.

## Rule card, front and back

Front, big enough to read on the couch:

> Swap two neighbors. If they do not make a line, put them back.
> A line is 3 or more in a row or column. Take them off. Slide everything down. Fill from the envelope.
> New lines caused by the fall are free.
> 4 in a line: flip the piece you moved, mark `—` or `\|`. Matched later, it clears that row or column.
> 5 in a line, or a T or L: flip it, mark `*`. Swap it with a neighbor to clear that color.
> Undo whenever you want. Once a level, you may remove one piece.
> You win by the card. Running out of moves means try again.

Back: jelly, ice, collect, and scoring, in four short lines copied from the tables above.

## Order of work

1. Cut the board and write the arrow. Play level 1 with sticker dots before making buttons.
2. Choose the five drawings. Put them on the buttons.
3. Play levels 1 through 4 yourself. Fix any rule you hesitated over, in the rule text, before you copy it onto the card.
4. Tune move counts and the two score targets.
5. Write the twelve sentences. Copy fronts and backs onto cards.
6. Pack the tin: board, bezels, envelope of buttons, cards in order, rule card, and the sealed envelope under the board.

If a rule only makes sense with you explaining it, it is too fiddly. Cut it. Levels 1 through 4 are the game. Everything from ice onward is optional color, and it is better to ship twelve kind levels with two obstacle types than a faithful Candy Crush.
