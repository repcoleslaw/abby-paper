# 02 Pocket Well

Back to [[00 Paper Apps]].

A solo well, portrait, about the size of a phone. She takes a shape, lowers it onto the stack, lifts off every finished row, and slides the rows above it down. Twelve levels, one for each month. The well and the frames are paper. The stack is tiles. The phone original is Tetris.

Working title until you rename it. Better names are private. The title on the box can be ordinary. The gray on the cards does the personal work.

Build this after [[01 Pocket Match]] has survived an evening in the hand. Same collection: one verb, a few minutes, solo, generous.

## Why this shape

Tetris feels good because of a short list of beats. A shape arrives. She already knows the next one. She seats this one. A finished row vanishes. The stack above it drops, and the holes stay holes. Sometimes the long piece finishes four rows at once, and the well is empty enough to breathe. The next shape is already waiting.

The clock is how the phone forces the decision. This collection already refuses the clock. Her hand is the drop. A piece budget does the job a timer would have done.

| Beat on the phone | On the table |
|---|---|
| A tetromino appears at the top | She draws a paper frame and holds it over the well |
| She slides and turns it while it falls | She turns the frame in her fingers and lowers it until it sits on the stack |
| It locks | She lifts the frame off. The four tiles stay |
| A full row vanishes | She lifts every tile in that row |
| The stack drops, holes and all | She slides each surviving row straight down. A tile stays in its column |
| Four rows at once | The long frame fills a one-cell gap, four deep. All four rows come off |
| The next piece | One frame lies above the well, where she can see it |
| Garbage already in the well | Gray tiles, set out from that month's card, before the first piece |
| The stack reaches the ceiling | The frame in her hand will not fit. The level is unfinished |

A solid wooden tetromino dies the first time a row has to leave, because that piece is also sitting in the rows above. The shape is a frame she lifts away. The well keeps four loose tiles. Same reason Pocket Match uses buttons.

Six columns, on purpose. Ten columns gives her enough places to put a piece that she starts hunting for the best one. Hunting is homework, and it is not how this game feels on a phone. Six is a glance.

One placement clears one, two, three, or four rows. Then the well is quiet. A fall that causes another clear is Pocket Match's beat. This game drops once.

## What you are building

One tin, taller than it is wide, that holds:

| Piece | Count | Make it from |
|---|---|---|
| Well | 1 | Heavy paper, 6 columns by 12 rows, cells about 15 mm. The well itself is about 9 by 18 cm. Leave a margin above it for the waiting frame. Arrow in the margin pointing at the near edge: that direction is down. A line under the top two rows marks the entrance. |
| Frames | 7 | Cardstock, one of each shape: the bar, the square, the T, the L, the hook, and the two bends. Windows big enough that the tiles sit in the cells and the frame lifts off. Draw each shape on its frame. She should not have to remember a name. |
| Tiles | 20 each of 7 colors (140) | Flat card, about 12 mm, so a row can slide without dragging its neighbors. One color per shape. A pencil mark or a letter on the back is enough if a color runs out. |
| Gray | about 40 | The same punch, left uncolored. Rubble. It is not a shape. |
| Pockets | 8 | A paper strip or eight small envelopes: seven shapes and the gray. She should not have to fish in a mixed pile. |
| Envelope | 1 | Holds the frames that are still in the bag. |
| Clips | about 20 | The piece budget. One clip returns to the tin each time a piece locks. |
| Level cards | 12 | Index cards. Front is the well and the budget. Back is one sentence. |
| Rule card | 1 | The turn. The long version can live in this note. |
| Envelope | 1 | Sits under the well. She opens it after level 12. |

Prototype before you cut a frame. Draw the 6×12 grid, slip it in a plastic sleeve, and play level 1 with four coins or sticker dots, the shapes sketched on scraps. If seating four dots and lifting a finished row feels good, the frames are worth cutting. Cut the square first and try it with real tiles. The bends are the ones that fight you.

This tin does not share pieces with Pocket Match. Color here means which shape just locked. A drawing on the tile would make the stack harder to read.

### The gray

The year does not go on the tiles. It goes in the rubble.

Each card that uses gray gets a small silhouette from that month: a glass, a route, a table, something she can name. Keep a stranger from being able to name it. She is the test, same as the five drawings on the match buttons. A dozen gray tiles is a short level. About twenty is a long one. Never set out a row that is already full.

The back of the card is still one sentence from that month. A place, a meal, a Tuesday. Not "I love you."

## Rules

### Setup

1. Lay the well with the arrow toward her.
2. Put gray tiles on the marked cells. If a row is already full, the card is drawn wrong. Move one tile. Do not start the level by clearing.
3. If the card lists an order, stack those frames in that order, first shape on top. Lay the second frame above the well. Leave the envelope closed.
4. If the card says the envelope, put those frames in, shake, draw one into her hand, and lay one above the well.
5. Count the clips onto the card.

There is no settle step. The well does not refill itself from the top. The only tiles that enter are the four she places, and the gray that was already there.

### A turn

She holds the frame over the well and lowers it until it sits on the stack or the floor. Every cell of the shape has to lie inside the well, on empty cells. She may shift it to any column first.

She may turn it in her fingers before it locks. A turn that would leave the well or land on a tile does not happen. The frame stays as it was. There is no kick off the wall, and no special twist. If it does not fit, it does not turn.

She lifts the frame off. Those four tiles are now the stack. That is the lock, and it spends one clip. The frame goes to a face-up pile beside the envelope. The frame that was waiting comes into her hand. She draws a new one and lays it above the well.

If the envelope is empty when she goes to draw, the face-up pile goes back in and she shakes again. The frame now in her hand stays in her hand. One set of frames is why the shape she is holding is never also waiting in the envelope. If that boundary ever feels wrong in a playtest, cut a second set of seven and always shake a full set of the shapes that are not currently out.

Until she picks up the waiting frame, she may put this piece back and try again. If a row was cleared, those tiles go back into the row first, gray included, in the cells they left. The clip comes back.

Once a level she may lift any one tile, gray or colored, and let the tiles above it in that column slide down to close the gap. Other columns stay put. The lifted tile goes to its pocket. If closing the gap finishes a row, that row comes off and the rows above move down as usual. This does not spend a clip. Write both permissions on the rule card. The one lift is how she deletes a tile that is in the way. Ordinary clears still move whole rows, holes and all.

### Clearing

A row is finished when all six cells hold a tile. She lifts the whole row off. Colored tiles go back to their pockets. Gray tiles go into the dish on the card and count.

Then the rows above the gap move straight down, until each one sits on the next row that stayed, or on the floor. Tiles in a row move together. A tile stays in its column. A hole stays in its column.

A drop never plugs a hole, and a falling stack never makes a new full row. If you slide tiles down inside their columns, holes fill themselves and this becomes Pocket Match. If a playtest keeps doing that by accident, give each row a strip of paper to sit on. She moves the strip, and the tiles come with it.

Two or three finished rows from one piece come off together, even if a surviving row sat between them. That middle row moves down with the rows above. Still one drop. Still no new full row at the end of it.

### The long frame

The bar is four in a line. Lowered vertically into a one-cell gap that is four deep, with the other five cells of each of those rows already full, it finishes four rows at once. All four come off. That is the moment the phone game is built around.

Level 4 hands it to her. Later cards can ask her to leave the gap herself. None of them should require a clever spin. The bar laid flat, finishing one row, is just a row.

### When the frame will not fit

If she cannot set the whole shape on empty cells inside the twelve rows, the level is unfinished. Set the card up again and add three pieces to the budget. Say that on the rule card in plain language. A clogged well is not a failed evening.

She can use her one tile-lift before she decides the frame will not fit.

### Goals

Use one goal per level.

| Goal | Done when |
|---|---|
| Rows | The tally on the card is met. A four-row clear counts as four |
| Gray | No gray tiles left in the well. They are in the dish |
| The long clear | She has finished four rows with one placement of the bar |

Leftover clips are not a score. Running out of clips with the goal unmet is the same as a frame that will not fit: set up again, with three extra pieces.

### Playing beside her

Default is solo.

If you want a role on the night you give it to her: you hold the envelope and slide the rows down after each clear. She chooses every seat.

If she asks to play together: one well, alternate pieces, shared goal, shared clips. Either person may point. A second well that dumps gray onto hers is the phone's versus mode. Leave it there.

## Left on the phone

The speed curve. Wall kicks, T-spins, and combo tables. Back-to-back bonuses. Holding a frame aside for later.

Add the hold only if a playtest feels cruel without it. One sentence, and it can wait: she may set the frame in her hand to one side and take the waiting frame instead. The swap is free. Do not write a level that requires it.

## The twelve levels

Play each one yourself before you write it on a card. The piece counts are drafts. If you finish with more than five clips left, add a little gray or cut three pieces. If the frame will not fit twice on a relaxed try, add three pieces. Err toward her finishing.

Any seat that fits is legal. A card that only clears if she finds the one perfect column is a puzzle, and it will feel like homework. Leave enough gray, or enough spare pieces, that a slightly messy stack still finishes.

Every card front: level number, which frames are in play, the piece budget, the goal, and a tiny map if there is gray. Every card back: one specific sentence from that month.

An order on the card means a fixed stack, first shape on top. "Envelope" means she shakes the named frames.

## Rule card, front and back

Front, big enough to read on the couch:

> Draw a frame. Lower it until it sits on the stack. You may turn it first. If the turn does not fit, it stays.
> Lift the frame off. The tiles stay.
> Any full row comes off. The rows above move straight down. Holes stay where they are.
> The frame above the well is next.
> Undo until you pick up the next frame. Once a level, you may lift one tile.
> You win by the card. A piece that will not fit means set up again.

Back: gray goes to the dish when its row is full. Four rows from the long frame count as four, and a card may ask for that and nothing else. When the envelope is empty, shake the used frames and keep going. Setting up again includes three extra pieces.

## Order of work

1. Draw the well and the arrow. Play level 1 with dots before you cut a frame.
2. If the rows keep falling apart into columns, add a paper strip under each row and play level 1 again.
3. Cut the square, then the other six. Play levels 1 through 4 yourself.
4. Fix any rule you hesitated over, in the rule text, before you copy it onto the card.
5. Replace the sample gray with shapes from the year. Tune the piece counts.
6. Write the twelve sentences. Copy fronts and backs onto cards.
7. Pack the tin: well, frames, pockets of tiles, clips, cards in order, rule card, and the sealed envelope under the well.

If a rule only makes sense with you explaining it, it is too fiddly. Cut it. Levels 1 through 4 are the game. The envelope, the buried gray, and the short bag on level 10 are optional color. It is better to ship twelve kind levels of seat, clear, and drop than a faithful Tetris.
