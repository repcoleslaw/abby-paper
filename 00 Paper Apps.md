# Paper Apps

A pocket collection of phone games rebuilt as paper, for a first anniversary. The traditional gift for year one is paper. She already spends her idle time on match-3 games, so the first app in the collection is that feeling, made into something she can hold.

## What to keep from a phone game

Copy the **loop**, not the picture of the screen.

A match-3 feels good because of a short list of beats: see a pair, swap one piece in, watch a line vanish, watch the column fall, get an unplanned second match from the fall. Levels are short. The next one is already waiting. Nothing important is lost if she puts it down.

Paper can do every one of those beats except the animation. Her hands replace the animation. That is the point, so the pieces have to be loose objects she slides, not marks she erases and redraws. A draw-and-erase grid dies the first time a column has to fall.

## Design rules for the whole collection

1. **Phone-sized.** The board should close up and sit in one hand. About the footprint of her phone, so the joke of the gift is obvious.
2. **Solo first.** She plays these games alone on the couch. A two-player mode can exist, but the gift fails if it only works when you play with her.
3. **One sitting is one level.** A few minutes, a clear goal, a move limit. A stack of levels replaces the "next" button.
4. **Generous on purpose.** No timer, no lives, no failed-level punishment. Undo is free. Goals get tuned until she can win relaxed.
5. **The personal part is the content, not a monogram.** Five token pictures and one sentence on each level, taken from the actual year. The rules stay simple enough that a stranger could play.

## What not to bring over

Lives, clocks, boosters you pay for, near-miss boards that are designed to make her fail, and obstacle types piled on top of each other. Those are how the phone game stretches a session and sells moves. A gift can just be the pleasing part.

## Later apps

Only if Pocket Match feels good in the hand. Same rules: one core verb, short sessions, solo.

- **Word grids** in the Wordle family: a paper row of five, a pencil, a tiny dictionary of words you actually use with each other.
- **Merge piles** in the Suika / 2048 family: tiles that combine when two equals touch, on a small tray.
- **Patience:** one deck, a one-card solitaire, a score card. Closest to paper already. Only worth making if you restyle it as one of the apps rather than handing her a deck.
